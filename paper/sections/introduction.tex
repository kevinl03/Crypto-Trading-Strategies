\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Cryptocurrency markets are fragmented across dozens of centralized exchanges (CEXs), each operating independent order books with heterogeneous liquidity, fee structures, and matching-engine latencies. Unlike equity markets, where consolidated tape systems and best-execution regulation enforce price convergence, crypto exchanges are isolated venues. This fragmentation creates persistent cross-exchange price deviations for identical assets~\cite{makarov2020trading}.

Statistical arbitrage (``pairs trading'') exploits mean-reverting spreads between co-moving instruments~\cite{krauss2017statistical}. In cryptocurrency markets, every existing pairs-trading study uses \emph{rule-based z-score thresholds} to generate entry and exit signals: trade when the spread's rolling z-score exceeds a fixed boundary, exit when it reverts~\cite{fil2020pairs, tadi2021evaluation, ko2023pairs, palazzi2025trading}. No prior work trains a model to \emph{predict where the z-score will be next} and uses that forecast as the trading signal.

We demonstrate that a gradient-boosting model (LightGBM) trained on live centralized-exchange microstructure features can predict the next-snapshot z-score of same-asset cross-exchange spreads, replacing mechanical threshold rules with a learned forecast. The model ingests 68~features spanning orderbook imbalance, trade flow, funding rates, open interest, and lagged spread dynamics across 23~volatile cryptocurrency assets and 6~exchanges at approximately one-minute granularity. A confidence filter restricts trading to predictions where $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$, acting as a learned abstention mechanism that selects high-quality entries from the forecast distribution.

\subsection{Dataset and Non-Backfillable Features}

A distinguishing feature of this work is the dataset itself. Our live collector snapshots 9~concurrent signal families every 60--90~seconds across 23~assets and up to 6~exchanges: ticker quotes (best bid/ask), L2~orderbook (top~20 price levels per side), recent trades, funding rates, open interest, withdrawal status, exchange operational status, precomputed spread matrices, and OHLCV candles. The asset universe was selected for high cross-exchange spread volatility and sufficient liquidity across multiple venues. Collection spans over two months of live windows (June--July 2026) at one-minute granularity.

Public exchange APIs serve historical OHLCV candles, but \emph{not} historical order books, quote-level snapshots, or trade prints. Every orderbook and trade-flow observation in our dataset exists only because it was collected live and cannot be reconstructed retroactively for any past window. This non-backfillable property means that the microstructure features central to our model are unavailable to researchers working with standard historical data sources.

The resulting dataset is released publicly through \datasetref. To the best of our knowledge, no comparable public dataset provides synchronized cross-exchange L2~orderbook, trade flow, and funding rate data at minute-scale granularity for volatile cryptocurrency assets. We consider the dataset release a contribution independent of the model.

\subsection{Contributions}

We make the following contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item We train a gradient-boosting model to \textbf{predict the next-snapshot z-score} of same-asset cross-exchange cryptocurrency spreads, replacing the mechanical z-score threshold rules used by all prior high-frequency crypto pairs-trading systems.

    \item We validate the model in \textbf{two live paper-trading campaigns} (8~hours each, weekday and weekend) with no future information leakage, reporting both forecast metrics (R$^2$, directional accuracy) and trading metrics (per-trade profit, hourly portfolio Sharpe ratio) with a matched naive persistence baseline on every evaluation.

    \item We show that the learned forecast achieves \textbf{directional accuracy of 76.9\%}, prediction \textbf{R$^2$ of 0.35}, and an \textbf{hourly portfolio Sharpe ratio of 2.41} on the live session, improving per-trade directional accuracy by 7.6~percentage points and mean profit by 71\% over a capacity-matched mechanical persistence baseline.

    \item We release a \textbf{publicly available dataset} of synchronized cross-exchange microstructure data (L2~orderbook, trade flow, funding rates, open interest) at one-minute granularity for 23~volatile cryptocurrency assets across 6~exchanges, collected over two months of live windows. This data cannot be reconstructed from historical exchange APIs.
\end{enumerate}
