\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Cryptocurrency markets are fragmented across dozens of centralized exchanges (CEXs), each operating independent order books with heterogeneous liquidity, fee structures, and matching-engine latencies. Unlike equity markets, where consolidated tape systems and best-execution regulation enforce price convergence, crypto exchanges are isolated venues. This fragmentation creates persistent cross-exchange price deviations for identical assets~\cite{makarov2020trading}.

Statistical arbitrage (``pairs trading'') exploits mean-reverting spreads between co-moving instruments~\cite{krauss2017statistical}. In cryptocurrency markets, every existing pairs-trading study uses \emph{rule-based z-score thresholds} to generate entry and exit signals: trade when the spread's rolling z-score exceeds a fixed boundary, exit when it reverts~\cite{fil2020pairs, tadi2021evaluation, ko2023pairs, palazzi2025trading}. No prior work trains a model to \emph{predict where the z-score will be next} and uses that forecast as the trading signal.

We demonstrate that a gradient-boosting model (LightGBM) can predict the next-snapshot z-score of same-asset cross-exchange spreads from live centralized-exchange data, replacing mechanical threshold rules with a learned forecast. The machine learning approach is motivated by the observation that fixed threshold rules are structurally unable to capture nonlinear dependence of future z-scores on the recent spread trajectory and venue-pair context~\cite{sarmento2024ml, shen2022arbitrage, perrone2026pairs}. The model is trained on 68~features spanning lagged spread dynamics, orderbook imbalance, trade flow, funding rates, open interest, and cross-venue dispersion across 23~volatile cryptocurrency assets and 6~exchanges at approximately one-minute granularity; a feature-group ablation (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}) shows that the lagged z-score trajectory dominates. A confidence filter restricts trading to predictions where $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$, selecting high-quality entries from the forecast distribution.

\subsection{Dataset and Non-Backfillable Features}

Our live collector snapshots orderbook, trade, and funding data across 23~assets and 6~exchanges at approximately one-minute granularity. Public exchange APIs serve historical OHLCV candles, but \emph{not} historical order books, quote-level snapshots, or trade prints. Every orderbook and trade-flow observation in our dataset exists only because it was collected live and cannot be reconstructed retroactively. This non-backfillable property means that orderbook, trade-flow, and funding observations are unavailable to researchers working with standard historical data sources. The resulting dataset is released publicly through \datasetref; see the repository for collection details. We consider the dataset release a contribution independent of the model---and, as Section~\ref{sec:ablation} shows, independent of whether a tree ensemble currently extracts incremental R$^2$ from those signals.

\subsection{Contributions}

We make the following contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item We train a gradient-boosting model to \textbf{predict the next-snapshot z-score} of same-asset cross-exchange cryptocurrency spreads, replacing the mechanical z-score threshold rules used by all prior high-frequency crypto pairs-trading systems. A nested feature-group ablation shows that this forecast is driven by the recent z-score trajectory and venue-pair identity rather than by incremental microstructure lift under the published tree protocol.

    \item We validate the model in \textbf{two live paper-trading campaigns} (8~hours each, weekday and weekend) with no future information leakage, reporting both forecast metrics (R$^2$, directional accuracy) and trading metrics (per-trade profit, hourly portfolio Sharpe ratio) with a matched naive persistence baseline on every evaluation.

    \item We show that the learned forecast achieves \textbf{directional accuracy of 76.9\%}, prediction \textbf{R$^2$ of 0.35}, and an \textbf{hourly portfolio Sharpe ratio of 2.41} on the live session, improving per-trade directional accuracy by 7.6~percentage points and mean profit by 71\% over a capacity-matched mechanical persistence baseline.

    \item We release a \textbf{publicly available dataset} of synchronized cross-exchange microstructure data (L2~orderbook, trade flow, funding rates, open interest) at one-minute granularity for 23~volatile cryptocurrency assets across 6~exchanges, collected over two months of live windows. This data cannot be reconstructed from historical exchange APIs.
\end{enumerate}
