\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Z-Score Units Capture Forecast Quality}\label{sec:proxy_gap}

The settlement proxy $\text{pnl}_{\text{proxy}} = \text{direction} \times
z_{t+H}$ measures the model's ability to correctly predict the standardized
spread level at horizon $H$. It is designed as a forecast quality metric, not as
an economic P\&L.

When the same validation $\tau{=}0.9$ ranked-capacity trades are re-scored in basis points of realized spread change, the ordering between policies inverts: the learned policy averages $-0.94$~bps per trade (34.9\% win rate), while mechanical mean-reversion yields $+3.97$~bps (76.0\% win rate). Table~\ref{tab:bps} summarizes this difference.

\label{tab:bps}

This inversion reflects the model's training objective: predicting the z-score level $z_{t+H}$ (dominated by the slow-moving rolling mean) rather than the instantaneous spread change $\Delta S_t$. The z-unit settlement is appropriate for evaluating forecast quality, which is the primary claim of this work. For production deployment, a direct spread-change target net of fees would align training with the economic objective.

\subsection{Portfolio Sharpe Characterizes Forecast Stability}

The validation hourly Sharpe of 1.90 under the $\tau{=}0.9$ ranked-capacity baseline book characterizes forecast stability but is not directly comparable to the multi-month, cost-aware benchmarks in the crypto pairs-trading literature~\cite{tadi2021evaluation, tadi2025copula}. The capacity-matched mechanical persistence peer attains a higher hourly Sharpe (2.62) while trailing on per-trade DirAcc and mean z-proxy, confirming that both policies exploit a high-persistence regime. Per-trade forecast quality (R$^2$ and directional accuracy) is the primary evidence for the model's advantage; Sharpe is a secondary stability indicator.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Evaluation horizon.} The ${\approx}$72-hour validation campaign is the primary live stress test. Headline metrics use the paper gate $\tau{=}0.9$ (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}); some validation $\tau{=}0.9$ numbers are post-hoc filters or ranked-capacity replays on collected sessions rather than a separate live re-fill at that gate.

\textbf{Proxy settlement.} P\&L is measured in z-units gross of fees, slippage, and bid-ask crossing (Section~\ref{sec:proxy_gap}). No dollar return is claimed.

\textbf{Execution latency.} The collector's observed cadence is approximately 110~seconds, so signals are acted on against market states up to two minutes stale. Fischer et al.~\cite{fischer2019statistical} show that minute-scale crypto alpha can vanish within 1--5~minutes of delay; we have not run the corresponding ablation.

\textbf{Dependence structure.} Rows within a snapshot share market state, so the 2.9~million training rows and the large test/validation prediction panels are not independent samples. We report no significance tests.
