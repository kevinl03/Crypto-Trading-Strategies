\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a gradient-boosting framework that replaces mechanical z-score thresholds with a learned forecast for cross-exchange cryptocurrency spread dynamics. On live paper-trading entries at $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$, the model achieves R$^2$ of 0.35 and directional accuracy of 76.9\% on Jul~31, improving per-trade accuracy by 7.6~pp and mean z-unit profit by 71\% over a capacity-matched mechanical persistence baseline; a $\sim$72h campaign remains consistent (DirAcc 79.0\%). The confidence filter lifts R$^2$ by 0.19--0.25 by selecting high-signal regimes; raising $\tau$ to $0.9$ further improves per-trade DirAcc/R$^2$ and is the first operating point with per-trade Sharpe ${\geq}1$, at the cost of roughly three-quarters fewer trades (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}). On filtered rows at $\tau{=}0.5$, the model's incremental value is primarily in capturing magnitude rather than direction. Critically, this is a forecasting result: scored in basis points of realized spread change, none of the policies---including ours---has a positive mean at any non-zero transaction cost (Section~\ref{sec:proxy_gap}). Section~\ref{sec:limitations} details the threats to validity.

\subsection{Future Work}

Incorporating realistic fee structures, execution latency, and a direct spread-change training target would bridge the gap between z-unit performance and economic profitability. The released dataset enables the community to benchmark alternative architectures---including sequence models that may capture temporal dependencies the tree ensemble cannot---on the same cross-exchange microstructure panel.
