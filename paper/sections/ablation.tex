\section{Ablation Studies}\label{sec:ablation}

\subsection{Confidence Filter}

The $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$ confidence filter restricts trading to high-confidence predictions. Table~\ref{tab:filter_ablation} quantifies its effect on R$^2$.

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Effect of the $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$ confidence filter on model R$^2$ across campaigns.}
\label{tab:filter_ablation}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Campaign} & \textbf{All R$^2$} & \textbf{Filtered R$^2$} & \textbf{$\Delta$} & \textbf{\% rows filtered} \\
\midrule
Jul~30 (live)  & 0.054 & 0.248 & +0.194 & 10.3\% \\
Jul~31 (live)  & 0.104 & 0.347 & +0.243 & 14.9\% \\
Offline test   & 0.133 & 0.383 & +0.250 & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The filter consistently lifts R$^2$ by approximately 0.2--0.25 across all evaluation settings, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:filter_lift}. Importantly, this lift is not free: the filtered subset constitutes only 10--15\% of all predictions, meaning 85--90\% of snapshot/pair observations are declined. The filter strategy parallels the ``skip low-magnitude predictions'' approach shown to improve profit-per-trade in Sarmento et al.~\cite{sarmento2024ml}.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig3_filter_r2_lift.png}
\caption{Model R$^2$ before and after the $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$ confidence filter. The filter selects predictions in high-signal regimes, lifting R$^2$ by +0.19 to +0.25 across both live campaigns.}
\Description{Grouped bar chart of model R squared on a vertical axis from zero to
0.35 for the two live campaigns. For Jul 30 the all-predictions bar is 0.054 and
the filtered bar is 0.248, a rise of 0.194. For Jul 31 the all-predictions bar is
0.104 and the filtered bar is 0.347, a rise of 0.243. In both campaigns the
filtered bar is roughly three times the height of the unfiltered bar.}
\label{fig:filter_lift}
\end{figure}

A critical caveat: much of the filtered directional accuracy is explained by persistence in high-$|z|$ states rather than incremental model skill. On Jul~31 filtered rows, naive persistence achieves 77.0\% directional accuracy versus the model's 76.7\%. The model's advantage is in R$^2$ (0.347 vs 0.174), indicating that it captures the \emph{magnitude} of future z-score movements more accurately than simply predicting that large z-scores will persist.

\subsection{Feature Importance: Role of Microstructure Inputs}

Figure~\ref{fig:importance} shows the top~10 of the model's 68~features by LightGBM split gain.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig4_feature_importance_top20.png}
\caption{Top~10 features by LightGBM split gain. Gain is overwhelmingly
concentrated in lagged z-scores and spread terms; microstructure features
appear only in the lower ranks.}
\Description{Horizontal bar chart of the ten highest-gain features, ordered
from largest at the top. The first bar, lagged z-score at lag one, reaches about
1,660,000 gain. Lagged z-score at lag two follows at about 1,040,000. Spread
in basis points at lag two, coin categorical, spread at lag one, and pair
categorical range from about 310,000 down to 190,000. The remaining four bars
are near-zero against the top features: trade buy-sell ratio lags for Kraken
and Coinbase, cross-venue mid-price range, and Coinbase orderbook imbalance.}
\label{fig:importance}
\end{figure}

The model is, to a first approximation, an autoregression on the recent z-score trajectory conditioned on asset and venue-pair identity. Lagged z-scores and spread dynamics dominate the ranking; microstructure and cross-venue dispersion features enter only in the bottom four positions. Only one of the ten highest-gain features is an orderbook imbalance term, and no funding-rate or open-interest feature appears in the top~10.

\subsection{Feature-Group Ablation}

To test whether microstructure inputs add predictive power beyond the lagged z-score trajectory, we retrain the model under a nested leave-one-group-in design on the Jul~25--28 held-out test set. Hyperparameters are held fixed at the published \texttt{LGBM\_PARAMS}; every variant uses the same boosting-round budget (the full-model early-stopping iterate, here 49 rounds) so that feature value is not confounded with round-count differences. The comparison metric is filtered R$^2$ ($|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$), the operationally meaningful regime. Feature groups follow the data-collection families; funding-rate and open-interest columns do not survive the null/weak-correlation prune and are absent from the trained feature set (consistent with the published 68-feature model).

\begin{table}[h]
\caption{Nested feature-group ablation on the Jul~25--28 offline test set.
All variants share the same LightGBM hyperparameters and a fixed boosting-round
budget. $\Delta$R$^2$ is relative to the autoregression (AR) baseline on the
$|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$ subset. Funding and open interest are marked pruned
because no columns from those families enter the trained feature set.}
\label{tab:logo_ablation}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
\textbf{Variant} & \textbf{\# feat.} & \textbf{R$^2$} & \textbf{R$^2$ filt.} & \textbf{DirAcc filt.} & \textbf{$\Delta$R$^2$ filt.} \\
\midrule
AR baseline (lags + momentum + identity) & 11 & 0.132 & 0.392 & 78.9\% & --- \\
+ ticker (mid, BA, volumes)              & 42 & 0.133 & 0.382 & 78.4\% & $-$0.010 \\
+ orderbook imbalance                    & 45 & 0.132 & 0.378 & 78.2\% & $-$0.014 \\
+ trade flow                             & 48 & 0.132 & 0.382 & 78.4\% & $-$0.010 \\
+ funding rate                           & --- & --- & --- & --- & pruned \\
+ open interest                          & --- & --- & --- & --- & pruned \\
+ cross-venue aggregates (full)          & 62 & 0.133 & 0.380 & 78.1\% & $-$0.012 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The AR baseline---lagged z-scores and spreads, momentum/acceleration terms, and the \texttt{coin}/\texttt{pair} categoricals---attains the highest filtered R$^2$ (0.392). Adding ticker, orderbook, trade-flow, or cross-venue groups does not improve filtered R$^2$; the full model sits 0.012 below the baseline. A complementary leave-one-group-out check confirms the same conclusion: dropping the baseline group collapses filtered R$^2$ from 0.381 to 0.124, whereas dropping ticker, orderbook, trades, or cross-venue features leaves filtered R$^2$ unchanged or slightly higher. Under this tree-ensemble protocol, the non-backfillable microstructure inputs are not predictively necessary for next-snapshot z-score forecasting. The live dataset remains a contribution---it enables architectures (e.g., sequence models over orderbook time series) that may use these signals more effectively than a gradient-boosted tree---but the claim that they are required for the published LightGBM forecast is not supported.
