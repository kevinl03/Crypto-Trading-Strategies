\section{Model Tuning \& Ablation Studies}\label{sec:ablation}

\subsection{Selecting Window Length and Confidence Threshold}

\paragraph{Window $w$.}
Increasing $w$ enriches $(\hat{\mu}_w,\hat{\sigma}_w)$ but forces longer warmup before a valid $z$ exists and slows $\sigma$ adaptation. Spreads-only LightGBM sweeps on the test set show skill rising into a plateau near $w{\approx}560$--$720$, with only modest gains past the production choice $w{=}300$ (${\approx}{+}1$\,pp DirAcc and ${+}0.017$ R$^2$ at $w{=}720$ for ${\sim}2.4{\times}$ lookback). Figure~\ref{fig:w_sensitivity} makes the trade-off explicit under the paper gate $|\hat{z}|\geq 0.9$: past $w{=}300$, further increases mainly buy longer context for marginal filtered DirAcc. We therefore retain $w{=}300$.

\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/w_vs_r2_diracc.png}
\caption{Z-score window $w$ vs test $R^2$ and directional accuracy under $|\hat{z}|\geq 0.9$. Past $w{=}300$, additional lookback mainly increases required context while DirAcc gains flatten.}
\Description{Two-panel line chart of test R-squared and directional accuracy versus z-score window W. Curves for all predictions and for absolute prediction at least 0.9 both rise then plateau; a dashed vertical line marks W=300.}
\label{fig:w_sensitivity}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Threshold $\tau$.}
Early live collection used a permissive gate $\tau{=}0.5$ to maximize coverage under $\text{max\_open}{=}50$. That choice is a starting point for selection, not the paper operating point. A post-hoc sweep on validation closes ($w{=}300$) shows that raising $\tau$ from $0.5$ to $1.0$ monotonically improves DirAcc, R$^2$, mean z-proxy, and per-trade Sharpe $\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{pnl\_proxy})/\mathrm{std}(\mathrm{pnl\_proxy})$ (Rf~$=0$), while cutting $n$ (Table~\ref{tab:tau_sensitivity}). \textbf{Per-trade} Sharpe first reaches ${\geq}1$ at $\tau{=}0.9$: DirAcc 86.7\%, R$^2$ 0.599, mean z-proxy $+1.37$, Sharpe 1.08 on $n{=}12{,}795$ closes---still a large book relative to $\tau{=}1.0$ ($n{=}9{,}390$), with a clear lift versus $\tau{=}0.5$ ($n{=}50{,}690$; DirAcc 79.0\%; Sharpe/trade 0.75). Neighboring points bound the choice: $\tau{=}0.75$ remains below Sharpe~$1$, while $\tau{=}1.0$ adds limited Sharpe for another ${\sim}27\%$ coverage cut. We therefore fix $\tau{=}0.9$ as the paper protocol; headline results use $|\hat{z}|\geq 0.9$ (including post-hoc filters on live books filled at a lower $\tau$).

\subsection{Feature Importance}

Figure~\ref{fig:importance} shows the top~10 of the model's 68~features ranked by LightGBM split gain.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/fig4_feature_importance_top20.png}
\caption{Top~10 features by LightGBM split gain.}
\Description{Horizontal bar chart of the ten highest-gain features, ordered
from largest at the top. The first bar, lagged z-score at lag one, reaches about
1,660,000 gain. Lagged z-score at lag two follows at about 1,040,000. Spread

\label{fig:importance}
\end{figure}

The model mainly relies on z-score and spread lags. This semi-autoregressive structure makes sense given the problem context, wherein the previous states are strong indicators of future motion of the spread and z-scores. The coin and pair categorical variables are also included in the top~10, indicating that the model learns to differentiate between different coins on different exchanges. Finally, the microstructure features, namely trade data and L2 order book data, are also included in the top~10. Even though they are of smaller importance, these features provide some insight to the model.

\subsection{Ablation: Pre-Training Feature Engineering vs.\ Raw Market Features}

The prediction target is $y_t = z_{t+1}$ ($H{=}1$). We ask whether
\emph{transforming} the same live microstructure---mid prices into spreads,
rolling $z$-scores, lags, and short momentum/acceleration---improves filtered
skill relative to training on raw live columns alone.

\paragraph{Engineered trajectory.}
From venue mids we form pairwise spreads $S$ (bps) and rolling z-scores $z$,
then construct lagged $S$ and $z$, plus short momentum and acceleration of those
series (9 columns). This is a pre-training transform of collected market data,
not a change to what the collector observes.

\paragraph{Raw market features.}
Ticker mids and bid--ask widths, L2 imbalance, trade volumes, and cross-venue
aggregates (51 surviving columns)---the same session's live signals without that
trajectory transform.

\paragraph{Identity controls.}
\texttt{coin} and \texttt{pair} are held fixed on both sides of the comparison.
They are not the engineering question: \texttt{coin} selects which asset's
dynamics apply; \texttt{pair} selects which two venues the cross-exchange
dislocation (and mean reversion) lives on.

We retrain LightGBM on the test set under fixed published
\texttt{LGBM\_PARAMS} and a shared boosting-round budget (49 rounds). Filtered
metrics use $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.9$ (Table~\ref{tab:tau_sensitivity}).

\paragraph{Result.}
The gain comes from transforming the initial microstructure, not from seeing
different data. Compared to non-engineered microstructure features
(raw ticker/OB/trade/cross columns with identity fixed), the
mid$\to$spread$\to z$ trajectory improves filtered directional accuracy by
\textbf{$+7.6$\,pp} (77.8\%$\to$85.4\%) and filtered R$^2$ by
\textbf{$+0.523$} (0.012$\to$0.535). Under the paper gate, pre-training
feature engineering on live mids is what moves next-snapshot $z$ prediction,
and the same lift carries through to higher mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ and
per-trade Sharpe.
