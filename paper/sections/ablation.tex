\section{Ablation Studies}\label{sec:ablation}

\subsection{Selecting Window Length and Confidence Threshold}

\paragraph{Window $w$.}
Increasing $w$ enriches $(\hat{\mu}_w,\hat{\sigma}_w)$ but forces longer warmup before a valid $z$ exists and slows $\sigma$ adaptation. Offline spreads-only LightGBM sweeps on the Jul~25 holdout show test skill rising into a plateau near $w{\approx}560$--$720$, with only modest gains past the production choice $w{=}300$ (${\approx}{+}1$\,pp DirAcc and ${+}0.017$ R$^2$ at $w{=}720$ for ${\sim}2.4{\times}$ lookback). A $w{\times}\tau$ grid scored on the Jul~31 collector likewise does not favor larger $w$ at matched $\tau$. We therefore retain $w{=}300$.

\paragraph{Threshold $\tau$.}
Early live collection used a permissive gate $\tau{=}0.5$ to maximize coverage under $\text{max\_open}{=}50$. That choice is a starting point for selection, not the paper operating point: on Jul~31 signal rows it yields DirAcc 76.7\%, R$^2$ 0.347, mean z-proxy $+0.75$, and per-trade Sharpe only 0.67 ($n{=}8{,}775$). A fine sweep on the same panel ($w{=}300$) shows that raising $\tau$ monotonically improves DirAcc, R$^2$, mean z-proxy, and per-trade Sharpe $\mathrm{mean}(\mathrm{pnl\_proxy})/\mathrm{std}(\mathrm{pnl\_proxy})$ (Rf~$=0$). \textbf{Per-trade} Sharpe first reaches ${\geq}1$ at $\tau{=}0.9$ (Table~\ref{tab:tau_sensitivity}): DirAcc 84.9\%, R$^2$ 0.514, mean z-proxy $+1.20$, Sharpe 1.02 on $n{=}2{,}026$ rows---still a large book relative to $\tau{=}1.0$ ($n{=}1{,}426$), with mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ already high versus $\tau{=}0.5$. Neighboring points bound the choice: $\tau{=}0.85$ remains below Sharpe~$1$, while $\tau{=}1.0$ adds little Sharpe for another ${\sim}30\%$ coverage cut. The same $\tau{=}0.9$ cut on Campaign~B closes confirms the lift from the initial $\tau{=}0.5$ book at longer horizon (50{,}690${\to}$12{,}795; DirAcc 79.0\%${\to}$86.7\%; Sharpe/trade 0.75${\to}$1.08). We therefore fix $\tau{=}0.9$ as the paper protocol; headline results use this gate (Section~\ref{sec:limitations} notes when live fills are post-hoc filtered).

\subsection{Feature Importance}

Figure~\ref{fig:importance} shows the top~10 of the model's 68~features ranked by LightGBM split gain.

\label{fig:importance}
\end{figure}

The model mainly relies on z-score and spread lags. This semi-autoregressive structure makes sense given the problem context, wherein the previous states are strong indicators of future motion of the spread and z-scores. The coin and pair categorical variables are also included in the top~10, indicating that the model learns to differentiate between different coins on different exchanges. Finally, the microstructure features, namely trade data and L2 order book data, are also included in the top~10. Even though they are of smaller importance, these features provide some insight to the model.

\subsection{Ablation: Pre-Training Feature Engineering vs.\ Raw Market Features}

The prediction target is $y_t = z_{t+1}$ ($H{=}1$). We ask whether
\emph{transforming} the same live microstructure---mid prices into spreads,
rolling $z$-scores, lags, and short momentum/acceleration---improves filtered
skill relative to training on raw live columns alone.

\paragraph{Engineered trajectory.}
From venue mids we form pairwise spreads $S$ (bps) and rolling z-scores $z$,
then construct lagged $S$ and $z$, plus short momentum and acceleration of those
series (9 columns). This is a pre-training transform of collected market data,
not a change to what the collector observes.

\paragraph{Raw market features.}
Ticker mids and bid--ask widths, L2 imbalance, trade volumes, and cross-venue
aggregates (51 surviving columns)---the same session's live signals without that
trajectory transform.

\paragraph{Identity controls.}
\texttt{coin} and \texttt{pair} are held fixed on both sides of the comparison.
They are not the engineering question: \texttt{coin} selects which asset's
dynamics apply; \texttt{pair} selects which two venues the cross-exchange
dislocation (and mean reversion) lives on.

We retrain LightGBM on the Jul~25--28 offline holdout under fixed published
\texttt{LGBM\_PARAMS} and a shared boosting-round budget (49 rounds). Filtered
metrics use $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.9$ (Table~\ref{tab:tau_sensitivity}).

\paragraph{Result.}
The gain comes from transforming the initial microstructure, not from seeing
different data. With identity held fixed, the mid$\to$spread$\to z$ trajectory
reaches DirAcc 85.4\% versus 77.8\% for raw ticker/OB/trade/cross columns
alone---a \textbf{$+7.6$\,pp} lift in filtered directional accuracy---while
filtered R$^2$ rises from 0.012 to 0.535. Engineered features without identity
remain strong (DirAcc$_f$ 84.7\%). Under the paper gate, pre-training feature
engineering on live mids is what moves next-snapshot $z$ prediction.
