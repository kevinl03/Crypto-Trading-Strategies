\begin{abstract}
Identical cryptocurrencies often trade at different prices across exchanges. Most high-frequency pairs strategies still turn that gap into a trade with a simple rule: enter when the rolling z-score of the spread is large, and wait for mean reversion. We show that a better signal is to \emph{predict} the next z-score. Using LightGBM on live orderbook imbalance, trade flow, funding rates, and open interest---features that cannot be rebuilt from historical candle APIs---we forecast the next-snapshot cross-exchange z-score for the same asset across venues, and trade only when the forecast is confidently large ($|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$). We test the policy out of sample in live paper trading on real market data, in both short runs ($\sim$8~hours) and a longer $\sim$73-hour campaign at roughly one-minute resolution. Trades are scored in z-score units, not dollars: a trade is a win if the realized next z-score moves in the predicted direction, and the per-trade score is $\texttt{pnl\_proxy} = \mathrm{sign}(\hat{z})\times z_{\mathrm{exit}}$. Over the multi-day run, about 79\% of settled trades are directional wins, with mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ of $+0.84$ across 50{,}690 closes; summed paper P\&L in these z-units is positive in every UTC hour of the session. Shorter campaigns show the same pattern (76.9\% directional wins; prediction R$^2$ of 0.35 on filtered entries) and, under matched capacity, beat mechanical z-score persistence by 7.6~percentage points in win rate and 71\% in mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$. We publicly release the code repository, trained model, and synchronized multi-exchange microstructure dataset on Hugging Face (\coderef; \datasetref) so others can reproduce and extend the work.
\end{abstract}
