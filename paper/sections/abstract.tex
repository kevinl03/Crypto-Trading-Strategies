\begin{abstract}
Cross-exchange price spreads for identical cryptocurrency assets create short-horizon statistical-arbitrage opportunities, yet high-frequency systems typically trade them with mechanical z-score thresholds on the current spread state. We instead formulate the problem as supervised forecasting of the \emph{next-snapshot} cross-exchange z-score, using a LightGBM panel model conditioned on live centralized-exchange microstructure---including L2 orderbook imbalance, trade flow, funding, and open interest---together with coin and venue-pair categoricals. These features require live collection and cannot be reconstructed from historical OHLCV APIs; we release the synchronized multi-exchange dataset as a standalone contribution. A confidence filter trades only when $|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$, selecting high-magnitude forecasts rather than acting on every snapshot. We evaluate out of sample in live paper-trading under the system's real collection and decision latency, spanning both short campaigns ($\sim$8~hours) and a multi-day campaign ($\sim$73~hours at approximately one-minute granularity), all on live-collected market data held out from training. On the multi-day session the filtered policy achieves approximately 79\% directional accuracy, mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ of $+0.84$, and an hourly portfolio Sharpe of 4.38, with positive z-proxy portfolio returns in every observed hour (74/74); shorter sessions show the same qualitative pattern (76.9\% directional accuracy, hourly Sharpe 2.41). Relative to a capacity-matched mechanical persistence baseline under identical settlement rules, the learned policy improves per-trade directional accuracy by 7.6~percentage points and mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ by 71\% on the primary short campaign. These results support learned next-$z$ forecasting with live microstructure as a stronger trading signal than matched z-threshold rules in this setting.
\end{abstract}
