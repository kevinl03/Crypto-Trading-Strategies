\begin{abstract}
Identical cryptocurrencies often trade at different prices across exchanges. Most high-frequency pairs strategies still turn that gap into a trade with a simple rule: enter when the rolling z-score of the spread is large, and wait for mean reversion. We show that a better signal is to \emph{predict} the next z-score. Using LightGBM on live orderbook imbalance, trade flow, and lagged spread dynamics---including L2 signals that cannot be rebuilt from historical candle APIs---we forecast the next-snapshot cross-exchange z-score for the same asset across venues, and trade only when the forecast is confidently large ($|\hat{z}| \geq 0.9$). We choose this gate as the first threshold where per-trade Sharpe reaches ${\geq}1$ while retaining high mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ without cutting the book as aggressively as stricter neighbors. We test the policy out of sample on a held-out test set and validate in live paper trading on real CEX market data over a ${\approx}$72-hour campaign at roughly one-minute resolution. Trades are scored in z-score units, not dollars: a trade is a win if the realized next z-score moves in the predicted direction, and the per-trade score is $\texttt{pnl\_proxy} = \mathrm{sign}(\hat{z})\times z_{\mathrm{exit}}$. Under the $|\hat{z}|\geq 0.9$ protocol on the validation book, about 87\% of settled trades are directional wins, with mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ of $+1.37$ across 12{,}795 closes. Under matched capacity at the same gate, the learned policy beats mechanical z-score persistence by 15.4~percentage points in win rate and 63\% in mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$. We publicly release the code repository, trained model, and synchronized multi-exchange microstructure dataset (including orderbook, trades, funding, and open interest) on Hugging Face (\coderef; \datasetref) so others can reproduce and extend the work.
\end{abstract}
