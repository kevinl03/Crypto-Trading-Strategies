\begin{abstract}
Identical cryptocurrencies often trade at different prices across exchanges. Most high-frequency pairs strategies still turn that gap into a trade with a simple rule: enter when the rolling z-score of the spread is large, and wait for mean reversion. We show that a better signal is to \emph{predict} the next z-score. Using LightGBM on live orderbook imbalance, trade flow, funding rates, and open interest---features that cannot be rebuilt from historical candle APIs---we forecast the next-snapshot cross-exchange z-score for the same asset across venues, and trade only when the forecast is confidently large ($|\hat{z}| \geq 0.5$). We test the policy out of sample in live paper trading on real market data, in both short runs ($\sim$8~hours) and a longer $\sim$73-hour campaign at roughly one-minute resolution. Over the multi-day run the model reaches about 79\% directional accuracy, mean per-trade $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$ of $+0.84$, and an hourly portfolio Sharpe of 4.38, with every clock hour positive (74/74). The shorter campaigns tell the same story (76.9\% directional accuracy; prediction R$^2$ of 0.35; hourly Sharpe 2.41) and, under matched capacity, beat mechanical z-score persistence by 7.6~percentage points in directional accuracy and 71\% in mean $\texttt{pnl\_proxy}$. We release the synchronized multi-exchange microstructure dataset so others can reproduce and extend the work.
\end{abstract}
